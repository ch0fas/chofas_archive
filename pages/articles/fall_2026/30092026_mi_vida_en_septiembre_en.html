I want to touch a Papyrus NOW

Halfway through the month I began reading El infinito en un junco by philologist Irene Vallejo, a book about the history of books that my mom recommended to me. My mom generally just recommends me books very casually but this time she went out of her way to deliver it to me directly in my room, a sign that it was really important for her for me to read this.

I'm not very far into it (approximately 1/5th as of writing) but I am enjoying a lot. It would be very boring if this was just a super-meticulously written history of books with no grace, but Irene tells the story while mixing together her own perosnal experience with books throughout her life (with a lot of detail, clearly) and this makes all that historical meticulousness super interesting and valuable. I wouldn't have thought to read a book on this topic but I am really glad I did. I'll report back when I eventually finish it.

The Well Of Loneliness

My novel of the month has been The Well Of Loneliness by Radclyffe Hall, which I've been reading since August. Taking place between the end of the 19th century and the early 20th century, the novel tells the story of Stephen Gordon, a lesbian (or "invert" following the terminology of the times) and the turbulence in her life as a result, as well as her interaction with historically significant events like the First World War.

Radclyffe Hall herself (who published this novel in 1928 to heavy backlash) was gay and this novel is a not-so-subtle projection of her own life. The detail in which Radclyffe expresses the indignation of Stephen (and her own, of course) to a society that doesn't accept women like her, her very complex bond with her mother, the romantic passion she expresses for other women and the love she feels from the few people in her life that actually accept her for who she is has so much sentimentality that I've cried more reading this novel than nearly any other I've read, I think. Of course it hits me on a personal level, as I see a lot of me reflected on Stephen, even living in an environment that's so different, which Stephen and Ms. Hall fought so hard for.

I am comparing Stephen with Radclyffe Hall a lot here because it's nearly impossible to not see her as a self-insert from her creator, and that's why I think it hits me as hard as it does. I don;t feel like I am reading fiction here: I am reading an autobiography, of a woman whose life reflects those of countless other men and women who lived and died in the shadow. A mandatory reading for anyone who considers themselves part of the LGBT community, and an enormous achievement in literature.

The Wyoming Sessions and more music

I've also heard a lot of new music this month, more so than in others.

My first honorable mention goes to K.T.S.E by Teyana Taylor. This album is a part of the famous Wyoming Sessions, one of five albums that were released in succession in the summer of 2018, either by or with executive production from Kanye West. I only heard this album just a couple minutes ago as of writing but it is just so G.O.O.D. Kanye is my favorite producer ever but I don't think I've ever heard this side of him: the love ballads in this album are so well crafted, they reminded me a lot of Slow Jamz, and if you add Taylor's beautiful voice and writing you get some truly truly special stuff. There is also a Paris Is Burning sample in the last track, which feels like a phrase that came out of a fever dream, and it is absolutely incredible. I cannot recommend this album enough.

I've been kind of obsessed with the Wyoming Sessions this month: I've been re-listening to ye, KIDS SEE GHOSTS and DAYTONA a lot in September, and all of these albums are just so well made from front to back.

My second honorable mention goes to Angel, the new LP from Lizzy McAlpine. I discover her music a few years back with her album five seconds flat which I love coming back to because it is very well written. I wasn't particularly excited for Angel because her last album, Older, was kind of mediocre in my opinion but Angel is a considerable improvement. Lizzy returned to her high quality of writing that she achieved in five seconds flat and even surpasses herself in some cases. My favorite songs were The light in the painting, Peach, A car wash and a new pair of pants, Anybody's Fool, Our Strange Ways, The moon, The crowd and Two elevens.

Other albums I heard in September are:

- Radical Optimism by Dua Lipa, a boring album by an artist that can only make singles and not full albums.
- SEPT 5th by dvsn, a very well-produced album that I've unfortunately not returned to that much this month.
- Take Care by Drake, the first album I hear from him since October of 2023 (and only the second in general). It has some good songs but it also has a lot of filler, and it suffers from the classic Drake-ism of having bars so atrocious that it ruins the entire song for you.
- Computerwelt by Kraftwerk, the second album I hear from this band. These guys really influenced Daft Punk and it really shows, I love these songs as background music for work.

Paris is almost as beautiful as Vienna

This month I saw Before Sunset, the sequel to Before Sunrise by Richard Linklater. I saw Before Sunrise nearly 6 months ago and it automatically became one of my favorite movies I've seen this year.

Before Sunset is a very necessary and ephemeral sequel, it has the same beautiful details and dialogues that Before Sunrise had, now taking place in Celine's natal Paris. These movies convince you that love is the scariest, most painful, most beautiful and most necessary force in the world.

Mulholland Drive

The other movie I saw this month was Mulholland Drive by David Lynch, a movie that never left my radar no matter what I did.

After it ented I spent around 90 minutes doing the best I could to digest it, and I think I succeeded, and I think it is pretty incredible. I did the best thing I could've done and saw this movie knowing absolutely nothing about it, and I will stop talking because I want to give you a chance to do the same if you haven't seen it yet, if you want to talk about the movie with me just let me know 🫡.

9/11

This month was the 25th anniversary of the September 11th attacks, an event that I am kind of always thinking about. I'll take this opportunity to share some articles I read this month related to the attacks, the twin towers and everything that came after they fell.

- "No Bigger Failure" by Josh Nathan-Kazis, on the international catastrophe from the supposed "War on Terror".
- The America That 9/11 Made by Jill Lepore, on the consequences of the attack here in America (the continent) and Asia.
- For a Photographer, the Towers Were an Ever-Present Focal Point by Keith Meyers, on the towers and their eminence.
- U.S. ATTACKED; HIJACKED JETS DESTROY TWIN TOWERS AND HIT PENTAGON IN DAY OF TERROR by N.R. Kleinfield, The New York Times's front-cover the next day. It is a time capsule of New York in the attack's immediate aftermath.
